NEW YORK STATE
HOUSING AND ANTI-DISCRIMINATION DISCLOSURE FORM

Federal, State and local Fair Housing Laws make it illegal to discriminate in housing on the basis of race, creed, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin. New York State Human Rights Law also protects against discrimination on the basis of age, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, marital status, military status, citizenship or immigration status, status as a victim of domestic violence, and lawful source of income.

It is illegal for a real estate agent to:

  • Refuse to rent, sell, or negotiate for housing
  • Make housing unavailable
  • Set different terms, conditions, or privileges for housing
  • Provide different housing services or facilities
  • Deny participation in any service related to the sale or rental of housing
  • Falsely state that housing is unavailable for inspection, sale or rental
  • Steer prospective buyers or renters to or from certain neighborhoods based on a protected class

If you believe you have been the victim of housing discrimination, you may contact:

  • NYS Division of Human Rights: (844) 862-8703 | dhr.ny.gov
  • NYS Department of State: (518) 474-4429 | dos.ny.gov
  • NYS Attorney General (Civil Rights): (212) 416-8250 | ag.ny.gov
  • U.S. Dept. of Housing & Urban Development: (800) 496-4294 | hud.gov

This disclosure is provided in accordance with New York Executive Law, Article 15 and Real Property Law §442-h.

NEW YORK STATE
DISCLOSURE FORM FOR LANDLORD AND TENANT

THIS IS NOT A CONTRACT

New York State law requires real estate licensees who are acting as agents of landlords and tenants of real property to advise the potential landlords and tenants with whom they work of the nature of their agency relationship and the rights and obligations it creates. This disclosure will help you to make informed choices about your relationship with the real estate broker and its sales associates.

Landlord's Agent: A landlord's agent is an agent who is engaged by a landlord to represent the landlord's interests. The landlord's agent does this by securing a tenant for the landlord's apartment or house at a rent and on terms acceptable to the landlord. A landlord's agent has, without limitation, the following fiduciary duties to the landlord: reasonable care, undivided loyalty, confidentiality, full disclosure, obedience, and duty to account.

Tenant's Agent: A tenant's agent is an agent who is engaged by a tenant to represent the tenant's interests. The tenant's agent does this by negotiating the rental or lease of an apartment or house at a rent and on terms acceptable to the tenant. A tenant's agent has, without limitation, the following fiduciary duties to the tenant: reasonable care, undivided loyalty, confidentiality, full disclosure, obedience, and duty to account.

Broker's Agent: A broker's agent is an agent that cooperates or is engaged by a listing agent or a tenant's agent (but does not work for the same firm as the listing agent or tenant's agent) to assist the listing agent or tenant's agent in locating a property to rent or lease for the listing agent's landlord or the tenant agent's tenant.

Dual Agent: A real estate agent acting as a dual agent must explain carefully to both the landlord and the tenant that the agent is acting for both, and what their respective interests are. Both the landlord and the tenant must provide informed consent in writing to the dual agency.

This form was provided to you by the listing agent or property representative. For more information, contact the New York Department of State at dos.ny.gov.
